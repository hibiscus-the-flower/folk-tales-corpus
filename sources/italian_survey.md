## HARVESTED EDITIONS

### italian_crane_en — Italian Popular Tales (Crane, 1885)
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg #23634  
- **Edition:** Thomas Frederick Crane; Houghton Mifflin 1885  
- **PD status:** 1885 ✓  
- **Tales:** 99 (Chapters I–VI: Fairy Tales, Stories of Oriental Origin, Legends & Ghost Stories, Nursery Tales, Stories & Jests; numbered I–CIX with some gaps)  
- **Notes:** Cornerstone scholarly collection drawing from Sicilian, Neapolitan, Tuscan, Roman, and other regional traditions. Crane cites Pitrè, Comparetti, Imbriani, Gonzenbach extensively. Title strip removes Roman numeral prefixes. Period titles preserved (incl. "CINDERELLA.", "BUCHETTINO." with trailing period).

### italian_basile_en — Stories from the Pentamerone (Basile, orig. 1634)
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg #2198  
- **Edition:** Giambattista Basile; translator unattributed on Gutenberg page (likely the 1893 Burton or 1848 Taylor translation — pre-1929 in either case) ✓  
- **Tales:** 32 (incl. frame story intro + conclusion; 30 actual tales)  
- **Notes:** Selection from the Pentamerone's 50 Neapolitan tales. Includes earliest known versions of Cinderella (Cenerentola), Sleeping Beauty (Sun, Moon and Talia), Rapunzel (Petrosinella), Puss in Boots (Cagliuso). Alternate Roman numeral h3 headings auto-skipped cleanly.

### italian_busk_en — Roman Legends: Fables and Folk-lore of Rome (Busk, 1877)
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg #48771  
- **Edition:** Rachel Harriette Busk; Sampson Low 1877  
- **PD status:** 1877 ✓  
- **Tales:** 93 (after removing Gutenberg colophon)  
- **Notes:** Rich Roman-dialect collection organized into Favole (fables), Filagranata (enchanted-thread tales), Legendary Tales, Ghost and Treasure Stories, and Anecdotes. Section dividers (FAVOLE., LEGENDARY TALES AND ESEMPJ., GHOST AND TREASURE STORIES…) had empty bodies and were auto-skipped. Some tales carry footnote digits in titles (e.g. "THE THREE LOVE-ORANGES.1") — these are Busk's footnote references in the heading, preserved in filenames.

### italian_straparola_v1_en — The Nights of Straparola Vol. 1 (Waters trans., 1894)
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg #75257  
- **Edition:** Trans. W. G. Waters; Lawrence & Bullen 1894  
- **PD status:** 1894 ✓  
- **Tales:** 25 (5 nights × 5 fables each)  
- **Notes:** Renaissance frame-tale collection (written c. 1550s); one of the earliest European printed fairy tale anthologies, predating Perrault by ~150 years. Tales are titled generically "THE FIRST FABLE.", "THE SECOND FABLE." etc. within each Night — files are distinguished by sequential number (001–025). Vol. 2 (Nights 6–8) not on Gutenberg; see catalogue-only leads below.

## COLONIAL-SOURCE ASSESSMENT

No colonial concerns for this harvest:
- Crane, Busk, Straparola/Waters: English/American scholars working with Italian sources at a time when Italy was an independent state, not a colonized culture. Crane explicitly defers to Italian folklorists (Pitrè, Comparetti) and marks his sources.
- Basile: Neapolitan author writing in his own dialect tradition.
- For future Italian-language collections (Pitrè, Comparetti, Imbriani): all native Italian collectors with no external colonial framing concerns.
