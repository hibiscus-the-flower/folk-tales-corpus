## HARVESTED EDITIONS

### french_perrault_en — Perrault's Fairy Tales (Harrap 1922)
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg #29021  
- **Edition:** Trans. Robert Samber & J. E. Mansion; Harrap 1922; illus. Harry Clarke  
- **PD status:** Translation 1922, well within US pre-1929 threshold ✓  
- **Tales:** 10 (Little Red Riding-Hood, The Fairy, Blue Beard, Sleeping Beauty, Puss in Boots, Cinderilla, Riquet with the Tuft, Little Thumb, The Ridiculous Wishes, Donkey-skin)  
- **Notes:** Classic literary fairy tales, originally published 1697 (French). This is the Harrap illustrated edition with Mansion's revised Samber translation.

### french_daulnoy_en — Fairy Tales of Madame d'Aulnoy (Lawrence & Bullen 1892)
- **Source:** archive.org `fairytalesmadam00dgoog`  
- **Edition:** Trans. Annie Macdonell & Miss Lee; Lawrence & Bullen 1892  
- **PD status:** 1892 English translation; NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT on archive.org ✓  
- **Tales:** 24 (Gracieuse and Percinet, Fair Goldilocks, The Blue Bird, Prince Ariel, Princess Mayblossom, Princess Rosette, The Golden Branch, The Bee and the Orange Tree, The Good Little Mouse, The Ram, Finette Cendron, Fortunée, Babiole, The Yellow Dwarf, Green Serpent, Princess Carpillon, The Benevolent Frog, The Hind in the Wood, The White Cat, Belle-Belle, The Pigeon and the Dove, Princess Belle-Etoile, Prince Marcassin, The Dolphin)  
- **Notes:** OCR harvest from a Google-digitized scan; OCR quality moderate. The largest English d'Aulnoy collection. Fortunée heading was OCR'd as "FORTUN^E". Running page headers embedded in prose — minor cosmetic issue only.

### french_planche_en — Four and Twenty Fairy Tales (Planché 1858)
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg #52719  
- **Edition:** Trans. J. R. Planché; Routledge 1858  
- **PD status:** 1858 translation, long out of copyright ✓  
- **Tales:** 24 (Perrault: Blue Beard, Sleeping Beauty, Puss in Boots, Cinderella, Riquet with the Tuft, Little Thumbling; Murat: Perfect Love, Anguillette, Young and Handsome, Palace of Revenge, Prince of Leaves, Fortunate Punishment; La Force: Fairer than a Fairy, Good Woman; Villeneuve: Beauty and the Beast; Caylus: Princess Minute, Impossible Enchantment, Bleuette and Coquelicot; Lubert: Princess Camion, Princess Lionette; de Beaumont: Prince Désir, Prince Chéri, Widow and Two Daughters, Prince Fatal)  
- **Notes:** Clean Gutenberg EPUB. Contains different translations of some Perrault tales than `french_perrault_en` — these are variants (different translations), not duplicates, and carry scholarly value.

### french_laboulaye_en — Laboulaye's Fairy Book (Harper 1866, trans. Booth)
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg #26386  
- **Edition:** Trans. Mary L. Booth; original 1866 (fr.), English trans. c. 1866; Gutenberg edition notes 1920 reprint (intro. by Kate Douglas Wiggin — that intro is copyright 1920 and was excluded)  
- **PD status:** Tales themselves 1866; intro skipped (possibly 1920 copyright). ✓  
- **Tales:** 10 (Yvon and Finette, The Castle of Life, Destiny, The Twelve Months, Swanda the Piper, The Gold Bread, The Story of the Noses, The Three Citrons, Story of Coquerico, King Bizarre and Prince Charming)  
- **Notes:** Laboulaye's original literary fairy tales inspired by Breton and French folk tradition. "On the Kerver" is a section divider (no prose body), correctly excluded.

### french_segur_en — Old French Fairy Tales (Penn Publishing 1920)
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg #30129  
- **Edition:** Comtesse de Ségur; trans. into English c. 1920; Penn Publishing  
- **PD status:** c. 1920 US translation ✓  
- **Tales:** 5 (The Story of Blondine/Bonne-Biche/Beau-Minon, Good Little Henry, History of Princess Rosette, The Little Grey Mouse, Ourson) — each is a multi-chapter novella-length literary fairy tale  
- **Notes:** Long-form literary tales, originally in French (1856–1857). Substantial word counts; useful for style and register comparison.

### french_cosquin_fr — Contes populaires de Lorraine (Cosquin 1886) [French]
- **Source:** Project Gutenberg #57892  
- **Edition:** Emmanuel Cosquin; 1886  
- **PD status:** 1886 publication, well before 1929 ✓  
- **Tales:** 30 (Jean de l'Ours, Le Militaire Avisé, Le Roi d'Angleterre et son Filleul, Tapalapautau, Les Fils du Pêcheur + Variante, Le Follet, Les Deux Soldats de 1689, Le Tailleur et le Géant, L'Oiseau Vert, René et son Seigneur, La Bourse le Sifflet et le Chapeau, Le Prince et son Cheval, Les Trocs de Jean-Baptiste, Le Fils du Diable, Les Dons des Trois Animaux, La Fille du Meunier, L'Oiseau de Vérité, XVIII, Le Petit Bossu, Richedeau, La Biche Blanche, Jeanne et Brimboriau, Le Poirier d'Or, La Laide et la Belle, Le Cordonnier et les Voleurs, Le Sifflet Enchanté, Ropiquet, Le Taureau d'Or, La Pouillotte et le Coucherillot, Le Foie de Mouton)  
- **Notes:** Major scholarly folk tale collection from Lorraine (eastern France). In French. Each tale followed by Cosquin's comparative REMARQUES (included in the same file). Cosquin was the first systematic French comparatist; these are authentic oral transcriptions. The EPUB had tales I–XVII in one file-set and XVIII–XXX in another (alphabetical filename order mis-sorts them; the title_strip removed Roman numeral prefixes).

### Quiller-Couch — The Sleeping Beauty and Other Fairy Tales (Gutenberg #51275)

### Spence — Legends and Romances of Brittany (Gutenberg #30871)
- **Reason:** Scholarly ethnographic book, not a tale collection. Legends are embedded in analytical chapters; would require bespoke parsing to extract individual narratives. Lower priority than Luzel/Sébillot.

### Sébillot — Le Folk-lore de France (6 vols) [French]
- **Reason:** Thematic reference work (organized by topic: sky, sea, fauna, flora…), not a tale collection. Not appropriate for per-tale HTML splitting.

### Cosquin — Contes populaires de Lorraine (French, Gallica / full 2 vols including comparative notes)
- **Status:** 30 tales from vol. 1 on Gutenberg harvested. Full 84 tales would need vol. 2 from archive.org. Future pass.

### Andrew Lang Color Fairy Books (Blue, Red, Yellow, etc.)
- **Reason:** Genuinely multi-cultural anthologies, not French-specific. French tales are embedded among tales from 20+ other traditions. Would require culture-tagging each tale to extract French ones cleanly. Better harvested as a separate "Lang" collection in a multi-culture run. Not within French scope.

### Perrault — Contes (French originals, d'Aulnoy Contes Tomes I–II in French, Gutenberg)
- **PD status:** Original 17th-century texts, absolutely PD  
- **Reason deferred:** Corpus already has strong English-language coverage of these works. French originals would be a valuable scholarly addition but were deprioritized in favor of getting breadth across languages. **Future pass if French-language original layer is desired.**

## COLONIAL-SOURCE ASSESSMENT

No heightened colonial concerns for this harvest:
- Perrault, d'Aulnoy, de Beaumont, Laboulaye, Ségur, Planché's selections: all French or Franco-Belgian authors collecting/writing their own tradition.
- Cosquin (Lorraine): French folklorist collecting in his home region; no colonial framing issues.
- Future collections to watch: Sébillot (Upper Brittany native, good), Luzel (Breton nationalist, explicitly anti-colonial framing in his approach). No concerns flagged.
