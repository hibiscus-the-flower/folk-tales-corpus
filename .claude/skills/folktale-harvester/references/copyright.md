# The public-domain gate

This corpus is **public-domain only**. Every text you add must clear this gate
*before* you harvest it. When a run is autonomous, you are the only check — so be
conservative: when in doubt, catalogue the metadata but do **not** harvest the
text, and note the doubt in the survey.

## The two rules that decide almost everything

A work is safe to harvest if it satisfies the US public-domain test (this corpus
is US-based):

1. **Published before 1929** → public domain in the US, no matter the author. (As
   of 2026 the cutoff is 1929; it advances one year each January 1.)
2. If published 1929 or later, it is almost certainly still under copyright in the
   US for our purposes — **skip the text**.

Two practical corollaries the project already relies on:

- **Project Gutenberg**: everything on the main gutenberg.org site is cleared for
  US public domain. Treat a Gutenberg edition as safe.
- **A full-view / fully-downloadable scan on archive.org** is normally PD —
  archive.org gates copyrighted works behind controlled (one-copy) lending.
  **"Borrow for 14 days" / waitlist = still in copyright → skip.** This is exactly
  why Weinreich 1988, Ausubel 1948, and the Ma'aseh Book 1934 were left out.

## Edition vs. work — the trap

The *story* may be ancient, but the **translation/retelling you are copying is a
separate copyrightable work**. A 2005 English translation of a Grimm tale is
under copyright even though Grimm died in 1863. Always date the *edition/
translation* you are about to harvest, not the underlying folklore.

Translator/reteller death dates matter for non-US "life + 70" reasoning, but for
this US corpus the pre-1929 *publication* date of the edition is the clean test.

## What to record

For every source you assess, write a line in the survey (see SKILL.md) with an
explicit verdict:

- **HARVEST** — edition pre-1929 or on Gutenberg; gives full text; how to split.
- **CATALOGUE ONLY** — exists and is relevant, but text is copyrighted or
  borrow-only; capture title/author/culture metadata, not prose.
- **SKIP** — not actually relevant, or duplicate of something already harvested.

Keep the reasoning. The project's history (shelving Lithuanian Andersen, skipping
Polano, holding the line on copyrighted mayse collections) is valuable precisely
because the *why* was written down.

## Metadata is always fine

Titles, author/collector names, cultures, ATU/type numbers, and bibliographic
facts are not copyrightable. The catalogue track can always record these even for
works whose full text you must not touch.
